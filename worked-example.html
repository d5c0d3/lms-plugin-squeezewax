A worked example

One record from turntable to “You own a pressing”. This is one way to do it: any ripper and any tagger works, as long as your files end up with the Discogs release number in a tag.

The record

Various – X-Mix-1 - The MFS Trip

A various-artists DJ mix on vinyl: 13 tracks over sides A and B.

Release
198756
Master
56108
01

Ripping

Turntable into a USB phono preamp, recorded in Audacity. One raw WAV per side was kept as a backup; each side was then split into tracks at labels and exported as 16-bit FLAC.

02

Tagging

In foobar2000 with its Discogs tagging component: select all the tracks, look up the release, compare, apply the tags.

By default that component writes DISCOGS_RELEASE_ID and DISCOGS_MASTER_RELEASE_ID. Here that gave release 198756 and master 56108 on all 13 files.

03

Collection, copy, rescan

The release was added to the Discogs collection, the files were copied to the music folder, and one ordinary rescan was run, with no “Sync now”.

The album then showed “You own a pressing” with both Discogs links (the master link comes straight from the tag), and appeared in “Records I own”.

What it looks like in the Material skin.

Why various artists didn’t matter

A various-artists record is the awkward case for matching by title, but it did not matter here: SqueezeWax checks the tagged release against your collection first, and only looks at artist names if that fails.

Other tagging tips

Good for LMS — not needed by SqueezeWax. What SqueezeWax itself reads is under Tagging for SqueezeWax on the overview.

Track numbers

Use a running number 1-N across both sides. LMS keeps only the first run of digits, so A1 and B1 both become 1. Keep the Discogs position in a separate tag if you want it.

Compilations

Set COMPILATION to 1 on various-artist releases and DJ mixes. Discogs’ “Various” is not LMS’s “Various Artists”.

ReplayGain

Add ReplayGain tags for level matching. LMS can apply track or album gain, chosen per player.
